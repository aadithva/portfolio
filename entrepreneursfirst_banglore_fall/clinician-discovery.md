## Questions Needed to Upgrade Discovery Into Proof

For every future or reconstructed conversation, capture:

1. What instructions are currently given to patients?
2. Where do patients become confused or fail to comply?
3. How often does this happen?
4. What operational or clinical cost follows?
5. Who owns the problem?
6. What has already been tried?
7. Why is the existing solution inadequate?
8. Would personalized video fit the workflow?
9. Who approves clinical content?
10. Who controls the budget?
11. What would a pilot need to prove?
12. What would they pay if that result were demonstrated?

## Proof Thresholds

Do not upgrade the claim until the evidence is documented:

- **Pain confirmed:** At least five clinicians independently describe the same
  recurring workflow problem with frequency or cost.
- **Pilot interest:** A named owner, patient cohort, workflow, and next action are
  agreed.
- **Would pay:** A decision-maker identifies a budget path or accepts a concrete
  price as plausible.
- **Committed pilot:** Written agreement or an active free pilot with an owner and
  cohort.
- **Outcome proof:** Baseline and post-intervention measures are recorded.

## Suggested Interview Wording

### Strong and accurate

> We have more than 10 clinician conversations across areas including US bariatric
> and weight management and luxury or cosmetic medicine. That gave us directional
> evidence about the communication problem. The stronger proof is IQRAA, where a
> kidney-transplant pilot has started.

### Do not use

> More than 10 clinicians validated the product and want to buy it.

The second sentence converts discovery activity into unsupported commercial proof.
