# EF Interview Rehearsal

## Objective

Make it easy for Divit to summarize Aadith internally in one sentence:

> Product founder with a repeated trust-and-communication edge, demonstrated
> resourcefulness, a durable team, real clinical access, and exact full-time
> commitment.

## Thirty-Minute Mock Interview

### Minutes 0-5: Founder

1. Tell me about yourself.
2. Why must you build a startup?
3. What is the most ambitious thing you want to achieve?
4. What is your individual edge?
5. What did your first startup teach you?

### Minutes 5-15: Contradictions

1. Why did you keep changing ideas?
2. Why healthcare if you are not a clinician?
3. Why are you still at Microsoft?
4. Why EF if you already have a team?
5. Would you change your cofounders?

### Minutes 15-23: Evidence

1. What traction do you actually have?
2. What exactly is happening at IQRAA?
3. Is Stanford live?
4. What did the 10+ clinicians consistently tell you?
5. Who will pay and from which budget?
6. What would falsify the thesis?
7. How did you get IQRAA to start a pilot?
8. What has the IQRAA pilot produced so far?

### Minutes 23-28: Founder Behavior

1. Describe the Inter-IIT turnaround.
2. What did the YC rejection change?
3. What is your biggest weakness?
4. Describe a decision where your cofounders disagreed with you.
5. What will the three of you do in the first 90 days full-time?

### Minutes 28-30: Candidate Questions

Ask one or two questions from `interview-brief.md`.

## Scoring Rubric

Score every answer from 1 to 5.

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Directness | Avoids the question | Answers after background | First sentence answers the question |
| Evidence | Abstract assertion | One general example | Specific, relevant proof with limitation |
| Individual edge | Hides behind team | Role is partly clear | Aadith's unique contribution is unmistakable |
| Ambition | Generic desire to help | Large company goal | Specific global outcome and expansion logic |
| Honesty | Inflates evidence | Mostly accurate | Separates proof, hypothesis, and aspiration |
| Pressure control | Rambling or defensive | Recovers eventually | Calm, concise, and willing to concede reality |
| Commitment | Future intention | Approximate plan | Exact dates and costly actions |

Target: no score below 4.

## Delivery Rules

- Speak at approximately 80% of normal speed.
- Pause for one second after the first sentence.
- Use no more than two examples unless asked.
- Never fill silence by adding weaker claims.
- If corrected, say "You're right" and update the answer directly.
- Do not mention that AI helped prepare the interview.
- Avoid startup vocabulary when a plain sentence works.

## Biography Compression Drill

Practice the same story at three lengths:

### 15 seconds

State only the repeated edge:

> I build products that turn complex technology into something people can
> understand and trust. I learned startup ownership in college, applied that edge
> to Copilot at Microsoft, and now apply it to patient communication at Medora.

### 45 seconds

Use four beats only: IIT Guwahati startup, Microsoft trust work, the healthcare
prompt that led from Owly to Medora, and current proof and commitment dates.

### 90 seconds

Add one story from `story-bank.md`. Do not add cycling, Inter-IIT, cofounder
achievements, Microsoft customer names, and market detail in the same answer.

If the answer exceeds 90 seconds without interruption, restart it.

## Pressure Drill

Have the mock interviewer interrupt with:

- "That did not answer my question."
- "What evidence do you have?"
- "Why is that your edge rather than your team's?"
- "That sounds like a pivot story."
- "Is Stanford actually live?"
- "Did anyone agree to pay?"
- "Why have you not already quit?"
- "What would make me reject you?"

The correct response is not greater volume. Restate the answer in one sentence,
give the strongest evidence, and stop.
