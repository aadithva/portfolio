## 1. Early Startup Ownership at DAOLens

**Use for:** Why startup, formative experience, failure, risk appetite.

> In my third year at IIT Guwahati, I joined a venture-backed Web3 startup building
> collaboration software for decentralized organizations. The
> learning rate was so high that I seriously considered leaving college. I learned
> how energizing full ownership is, but I also watched an ambitious product fail to
> find repeatable paid demand. That is why I now ask who owns the pain and who will
> pay before treating technical complexity as progress.

**Do not add without verification:** Exact funding, Nexus investment amount,
investor names, or an exact account of why the company closed.

## 2. Trust and Scale at Microsoft

**Use for:** Individual edge, why leave, enterprise product judgment.

> At Microsoft, I work on M365 Copilot experiences that help enterprise users
> verify and trust AI-generated answers, including citations and reference
> surfaces. I learned that a technically correct system can still fail if the user
> cannot understand where an answer came from or whether it is complete. Medora has
> the same product problem in a higher-stakes setting: approved clinical
> information must survive the handoff to the patient.

**Do not add without clearance:** Named Microsoft customers, confidential product
details, or unverified daily-active-user numbers.

## 3. From Owly Studio to Medora

**Use for:** Pivot, why healthcare, customer pull.

> With Hari and Adi, I built and sold AI-video work through Owly Studio. We learned
> that customers did not care which model produced a video; they cared whether the
> output fit a workflow and produced a useful result. A hospital contact familiar
> with that work asked whether the capability could be used for patient education.
> That contact was not enough to prove a market, but it was a stronger problem
> signal than discretionary advertising and led us into Medora.

**Do not add without verification:** Exact customer count, revenue, or number of
videos produced.

## 4. Earning the IQRAA Pilot in Person

**Use for:** Resourcefulness, sales ability, healthcare trust.

> Normal outreach was not getting us to the right hospital decision-makers. For
> IQRAA, we went in person and presented directly to a multidisciplinary clinical
> and operations group. We answered questions about clinical control and patient
> safety in the room and earned approval to begin a kidney-transplant pilot. The
> lesson was that healthcare trust is often built synchronously, not through a
> better cold email.

**Do not add without verification:** Exact attendee count, meeting time, payment
status, patient count, or a claim that patient videos have already been delivered.

## 5. Selling Across Time Zones

**Use for:** Commitment before resigning, distribution learning.

> While still working at Microsoft, I have been directly involved in outreach,
> hospital demonstrations, and calls with US clinics outside Indian working hours.
> Many calls reached automated reception or ended with a request to email, which
> taught me that access to the real workflow owner is a harder problem than writing
> persuasive copy. I have not waited for my final working day to begin learning how
> distribution works.

**Do not add without verification:** Exact clinic, call, or positive-response
counts.

## 6. Inter-IIT Strategy Change

**Use for:** Resourcefulness, learning the evaluation function, leadership.

> During the Inter-IIT Design Championship, our team was stuck in the middle of the
> rankings. I studied previous winners and how judges reacted, realized technical
> completeness was not enough, and changed the presentation strategy around the
> real evaluation function. We won Gold. The lesson was to identify the bottleneck
> instead of polishing the same solution harder.

## 7. Cross-India Cycling

**Use for:** Perseverance, self-discipline, operating under discomfort.

> I have cycled across India twice. One documented ride covered 3,909 kilometers in
> 16 riding days. It taught me that endurance is not one dramatic act; it is a
> system of showing up, recovering, and repeating the work every day.

**Do not add without verification:** Exact distance or duration for the first ride.

## 8. Cofounder Durability

**Use for:** Why this team, why EF with cofounders, commitment.

> Hari, Adi, and I have known one another for seven years and already built one
> company together. We know how each other behaves when customers reject us,
> production breaks, or strategy changes. Our roles are complementary: I own
> product, customer discovery, and the human experience; Hari owns security,
> privacy, and operations; Adi owns the video and systems stack. Hari and Adi
> become full-time August 1, I leave Microsoft at the end of August, and we plan to
> work together from Bangalore.

## 9. Stanford Quality Versus Speed

**Use for:** Cofounder disagreement, learning from feedback, product judgment.

> On the Stanford cesarean video, I wanted to keep polishing before we sent
> anything because the clinical context made quality feel especially important. My
> cofounders argued that without clinician feedback, we were optimizing against our
> own taste. We sent the version sooner, received specific feedback, and used it to
> prioritize the next revision. They were right about the decision point. I learned
> that quality is not maximal polish; it is reducing the riskiest unknown with the
> right reviewer.

**Do not overstate:** The feedback produced a revision plan. Do not say the
corrected version was delivered unless that has happened before the interview.

## Best 60-Second Founder Story

> I am Aadith, a product founder and designer from Kerala, currently based in
> Hyderabad. I studied design at IIT Guwahati and joined a venture-backed startup
> during my third year. It taught me that I thrive with end-to-end ownership, but
> also that technical ambition is useless without customers who will pay.
>
> For the last two years, I have worked at Microsoft on M365 Copilot experiences
> that help enterprise users verify and trust AI. In parallel, I built and sold
> AI-video work with Hari and Adi. A hospital contact familiar with that work asked
> whether it could be applied to patient education, which became Medora.
>
> Today IQRAA has started a kidney-transplant pilot, and Stanford has approved a
> collaboration that is not live yet. Hari and Adi become full-time August 1. I
> leave Microsoft at the end of August and plan to work with them from Bangalore.
